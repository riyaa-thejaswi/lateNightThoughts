LATE NIGHT THOUGHTS

A heart for a heart
— but you took two

A reflection on the warmth of late twenty-three, and the quiet ache of watching footprints sleep beneath the snow while drifting into cold yesterdays.

My parents once told me,

That one day I’d find,

A love so distinct,

I would softly call mine.

I never imagined

The boy all girls would chase,

Would crash into my orbit,

And become lost in my face.

You became my every sunrise,

The warmth in every light,

The pot of endless gold

At the end of my rainbow.

Those last few rapid months

Of late twenty-three,

Were the sweetest dreams I would ever see,

And they were mine for entirely free.

You promised that you loved me,

And oh you meant every word;

Those late night heartfelt confessions,

Those night-shrouded touches.

For a season of wonder,

I was your whole sky,

Before the spark flickered

And ran quietly dry.

You learned how to heal,

How to simply go,

To leave all our footprints

Asleep in the snow.

You drifted ahead

To a life unconfined,

While you left me in pieces,

Still lingering behind.

The cruelest part of all

Wasn’t the ghosted texts;

It’s knowing that your affection

Had truly fizzled out.

You gave me your heart,

Then you took mine away,

And now I am still trapped in the warmth

Of a cold yesterday.

WRITTEN IN THE DARK — R.
